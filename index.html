Facts about Treaty One

Treaty one was signed by 7 chiefs of different First Nations communities, and representitives of the crown. These 7 nations were the Brokenhead Ojibway Nation, Long Plain First Nation, Peguis First Nation, Roseau River Anishinaabe First Nation, Sagkeeng First Nation, Sandy Bay Ojibway First Nation, and Swan Lake First Nation. The representitives were Lieutenant Governor Adams G. Archibald, and commissioner Wemyss M. Simpson. They were supported by key negotiators and officials, which included Magor A.G. Irvine and James McKay.



Treaty one is not the only numbered treaty, there are 10 more numbered treaties. They are all in different areas, and all signed over a different part of Canada.

Locations of treaty territories

All the numbered treaties cover different parts of Canada. Treaty one and two cover southern Manitoba, which included the Red River Valley, and areas surrounding southern modern-day Winnipeg. Treaty 3 covers parts of northwestern Ontario and eastern Manitoba. Treaty 4 (1874): Southern Saskatchewan, parts of western Manitoba, and a small part of Alberta. Treaty 5 (1875): Central/northern Manitoba, Saskatchewan, and a portion of Ontario. Treaty 6 (1876): Central Saskatchewan and Alberta. Treaty 7 (1877): Southern Alberta. Treaty 8 (1899): Northern Alberta, northeastern British Columbia, northwestern Saskatchewan, and the area south of Hay River and Great Slave Lake in the Northwest Territories. Treaty 9 covers Northern Ontario and a small part of northeastern Manitoba. Treaty 10 covers Northern Saskatchewan and a small portion of Alberta. Treaty 11 covers Western Northwest Territories and part of the Yukon.
