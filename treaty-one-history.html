Treaty One History

Treaty One Information

Treaty one was signed on August 3, 1871 at Lower Fort Garry, Manitoba. It was the first of the numbered treaties between the crown and the Anishinaabe and Muskegon Cree peoples to be signed, officially establishing a relationship between the Crown and the Indigenous peoples of Canada that allowed for settlement in the region.

Treaty one was signed in order for the Europeans to facilitate peaceful, orderly settlement and agricultural expansion in the new province of Manitoba. It aimed to secure Crown access to land and resources while addressing Indigenous peoples' desires for a partnership, continued livelihood, and long-term security in a changing landscape.


Major Events in treaty one history

The signing of treaty one is not the only major event that happened between the Crown and the Indigenous peoples. There are numerous other things that happened prior to the signing of treaty one such of which include:

  • The Royal Proclamation of 1763: This Established that only the British Crown could enter into land agreements with Indigenous nations.

  • Peguis-Selkirk Treaty (1817): Lord Selkirk negotiated a treaty with Chief Peguis and other leaders for land along the Red River, establishing an early, yet often misunderstood, precedent for land payments in the region.

  • Transfer of Rupert's Land (1869-1870): Canada purchased the Northwest from the Hudson's Bay Company without Indigenous consultation or consent.

  • Red River Resistance (1869-1870): Metis and First Nations, led by Louis Riel, resisted Canada's unilateral annexation of the territory.

Life before the Treaty

Even before Treaty One was signed in 1871, the Canadian government was aready making advances to make the land their own. In 1870, the transfer of Rupert's happened. The Canadian government allowed the Hudson's bay company to sell Rupert's land which was a huge section of land, including much of western Canada. The first nations communities were not notified about this sale, and although treaty one was not established at the time, this was a huge power move that allowed the Europeans to take over a large area of land in treaty one territory.

This shows that even before the treaty, the Canadian government was already thinking of take control of most if not all the land in Canada, even though the Indigenous peoples were there first.
